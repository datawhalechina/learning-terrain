# Volume I: The Terrain of Learning

You read a sentence in the preface: **Force lets you calculate. Energy lets you understand. Geometry lets you see.**

That sentence is the foundation of the entire book. But a foundation is not a house. You need to know what will be built on it.

The three chapters of Volume I build the first floor—and the most critical one. Because if this floor isn't solid, the nine chapters that follow will feel like metaphor.

They are not metaphor.

---

Imagine you are blindfolded and dropped into a wilderness. You don't know where you are. You don't know where the lowest valley lies. The only thing you can feel is the slope beneath your feet—tilting left or right, steep or gentle.

Your task is to find the lowest point in this wilderness.

This sounds like a puzzle. But this is exactly what deep learning does every day. You **don't need** to know your absolute position in parameter space. You **don't need** to see the full panorama of the loss terrain. You only need to feel the slope under your feet—what we call the "gradient"—and take a step in the direction it points. Feel again. Step again.

Chapter 1 answers: **Why does this require geometry?** From Newton's force to Hamilton's energy, from Euclid's plane to Riemann's curved space—over two thousand years, the word "space" has undergone five transformations, each one closer to deep learning.

Chapter 2 answers: **What is moving? And what does it see?** Parameters are the body—the model stands at a position in parameter space. Representations are the vision—this position determines how the model understands the world. Every step of the body reshapes the structure of its vision.

Chapter 3 answers: **What does the ground beneath your feet look like?** The loss function is not a number—it is an energy terrain. The gradient tells the direction. The Euler step turns direction into displacement. Valleys, saddles, basins—every landform determines how far the model can walk and where it will stop.

---

Volume I may seem like fundamentals. But "fundamental" does not mean "simple." These three chapters establish the book's core logical chain:

**Parameter space is the body → Loss terrain is the slope → Gradient descent is walking → Representation space is the vision.**

Every link in this chain is not metaphor. It is mathematics. It is something that can be rigorously described with partial derivatives, Hessian matrices, and dynamical systems.

Are you ready? Chapter 1 begins with $F=ma$—but not the $F=ma$ you memorized.
